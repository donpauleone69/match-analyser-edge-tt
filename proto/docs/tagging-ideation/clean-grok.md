# Rally tagging

Contact times are already marked, so shot count and hitter are known. Hits alternate from the server, and who you are is set once per match. One tap per question, and Back undoes the last tap.

## Sequence

1. Who won? You or the opponent.
2. How the last ball ended. The score and who hit last pick the menu. A miss is Net, Long, or Wide. A winner is Forehand side, Backhand side, or Body. If nobody returned: Bounced twice or Missed in the air when the server won, and Into the net or Missed the table (long and wide together) when the server lost.
3. Your wing, only if you hit the last ball: Forehand, Backhand, or Elbow.
4. The receive, if there was one. Short: Push or Flick. Long: Loop, or Block or chop.
5. The third ball, only if a third contact exists and the receive was a push or a block: Attack or Control.

An untouched serve is two taps. The long path is five: you hit last, and the receive stayed safe.

## Why these fields

Who won is the score behind every strength and weakness. Winner versus error is not asked. It falls out of the score plus who hit last.

Where the ball missed, or where a winner landed, is the clip a coach would open.

Your wing turns a miss into a drill, such as backhand into the net. The opponent's wing is skipped.

The receive is the habit across a match. Each button pairs a length with an action, so a short loop cannot be stored.

The third ball is the next drill, and only while the receive left the choice open.

## Worked out or hidden

Hitter follows from the server. A winner hides the miss buttons. A miss hides the landing zones. No return hides receive, wing, and third ball.

Serve length follows from the receive: push or flick is short, loop or block is long. A double bounce is short. A miss in the air, or a serve fault, stores no length.

If the opponent hit last, your wing is skipped. Their winner already records your forehand, your backhand, or your body.

## Left out

Spin and serve names, because a phone will not show sidespin cleanly. Strokes and placement through the middle of the rally. Forced or unforced, quality, and intention, because the clip cannot check them. Lets, because a let is not a point.

## Controls

Full-width rows only. A toggle hides one answer, a dropdown costs a second tap, and a default would save the wrong score. No confirm button. The last screen is one sentence, and Back repairs it.

## Trade-off

The middle of a long rally is not stored, so it cannot be rebuilt shot by shot. What remains can be trusted: the score, how the point died, your wing when it was your ball, the receive, and the third ball when it was still a choice. That is enough for a strength, a weakness, a drill, and the right clip.
